This paper presents an AI-based Ecological Predictive Cruise Control (Eco-PCC) system for connected electric vehicles (EVs), designed to reduce traction energy expenditure through anticipatory speed management. Classical cruise control strategies: bang-bang and proportional-integral-derivative (PID) controllers react to speed error after it has accumulated, resulting in erratic torque profiles, missed regenerative braking opportunities, and unnecessary battery drain. To address this limitation, a two-layer predictive control architecture is proposed in which an outer LSTM forecasting module anticipates traffic and an inner control layer optimizes EV torque against the predicted reference.

A stacked LSTM network (128 -> 64 hidden units, Batch Normalization, Dropout p = 0.2) is trained on a 10,000-sample synthetic traffic dataset spanning four driving scenarios: highway, urban, smooth and variable, with scalers fitted exclusively on the training split to prevent data leakage. The network predicts a 20-step velocity trajectory from a 100-step historical window, achieving a validation RMSE of 0.41 m/s and MAPE of 2.1% after 50 training epochs.

Two LSTM-integrated controllers are proposed and evaluated against a bang-bang baseline. The LSTM-PID controller augments classical PID with a deceleration-only feedforward term that proactively reduces torque ahead of predicted speed drops without penalizing upcoming accelerations, a feedforward asymmetry principle that avoids wasteful preemptive motoring. The LSTM-MPC Controller solves a receding-horizon optimization problem via SLSQP over N= 10steps, minimizing a weighted cost function balancing speed tracking, normalized motor power (P/P_ref)^2, and torque smoothness, with LSTM forecasts serving directly as the referred trajectory.

Simulation over a 60-second variable highway profile (m=2500 kg, n_motor=0.92, n_regen=0.75, 48kWh battery) demonstrates that LSTM-MPC achieves 74.4% energy reduction and a speed RMSE of 0.149 m/s over the bang-bang baseline, while LSTM-PID delivers 58.4% energy savings at O(n) computational cost, making it the perfect candidate for embedded deployment. Results validate that data-driven traffic prediction, when correctly integrated into the cruise control loop, yields substantial and practically deployable gains in EV energy efficiency.
